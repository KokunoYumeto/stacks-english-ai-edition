# R34-R38: cohomology and commutative-algebra corrections

This checkpoint incorporates five admitted correction batches into the
cumulative, unofficial Stacks draft. It preserves the earlier FAC, FGA,
GAGA, Verdier, EGA, and errata work rather than replacing chapters with
isolated candidate files.

The changes repair cohomological comparison arrows, sheaf-cohomology
indices and references, and commutative-algebra arguments involving ring
indices, localization, parameter ideals, minimal primes, and direct
summands. The [changes browser](../CHANGES_FROM_UPSTREAM.md) places original
and replacement text side by side with the supporting evidence.

There are 77 newly accepted operations across three chapters. Exactly 76
change the cumulative source: the remaining correction was already
satisfied by an earlier FAC proof rewrite. One summation-notation edit is
an equivalent notational clarification, not a substantive mathematical
defect. The immutable admission record and its additive clarification are
both preserved.

Two independent builds produce the same 30 chapter PDFs: 3,005 pages and
32,046,922 bytes, with a fixed point after four sweeps. This is a selected
chapter package, not a newly rendered complete 116-chapter Stacks book.
References to chapters outside that build profile can still display `??`;
the build evidence distinguishes those external references from failed
internal references. In particular, Sites Cohomology page 102 has such a
reference to Sheaves on Stacks.

One documented layout residual remains: the corrected double-internal-Hom
triangle on Cohomology of Sheaves page 123 extends 16.28908 points beyond
the text width. It remains fully inside the physical page, legible, and
free of overlap or clipping. This is not a claim of zero overfull boxes.

EGA integration remains incomplete. Its public source-review frontier is
EGA I section 6.6.3; section 6.6.4 is being independently prepared as the
next proof-and-mapping checkpoint. Tohoku's historical proof dossier is
also not wholly incorporated into the readable source.

These are AI-written mathematical drafts, not proof-assistant-verified
results or work approved by the official Stacks Project. Validation
receipts record precisely what was checked; they do not certify every
mathematical claim.
